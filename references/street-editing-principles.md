# Street-photography editing principles

These principles are informed by official Magnum Photos material reviewed on 2026-07-16. They are not Magnum submission rules and do not authorize imitation of any photographer's personal style.

## Edit the visible frame

- Prefer the frame in which gesture and visual relationship are clearest.
- Examine edge collisions, competing faces, blocked gestures, mergers, reflections, empty gaps, and background explanations.
- Do not reward an unusual subject when timing or visual organization is weak.
- Allow mystery in the image, but do not manufacture importance with vague prose.
- If a photograph needs a long explanation to become interesting, lower the moment, meaning, or originality assessment.

Primary source: [Making the Image: Richard Kalvar's Woman in the Window](https://www.magnumphotos.com/theory-and-practice/contact-sheet-richard-kalvar-woman-window/)

## Keep people and surroundings connected

People and environments should be read together. A window, reflection, foreground object, architectural division, or patch of light matters when it shapes the relationship inside the frame, not simply because it is present.

Primary sources:

- [Alec Soth: Cleansing the Visual Palate](https://www.magnumphotos.com/theory-and-practice/alec-soth-cleansing-the-visual-palate/)
- [Photography and the Art of Looking](https://www.magnumphotos.com/theory-and-practice/photography-and-the-art-of-looking/)

## Separate observation from invention

Classify a claim before using it:

- `visible`: directly supported by the photograph
- `confirmed`: explicitly supplied by the photographer or reliable metadata
- `inferred`: interpretation not established by the image or photographer

Use visible evidence for scoring and criticism. Label confirmed context when it materially changes the reading. Do not present inferred identity, motive, emotion, social status, or relationship as fact.

## Ethical treatment

Magnum's Code of Ethics emphasizes honesty, accurate information, respect for privacy, minimizing harm, and special care with children and vulnerable people.

- Do not infer family relationships, occupation, ethnicity, religion, disability, housing status, or hardship from appearance.
- Avoid humiliating jokes or labels aimed at a subject's body, clothing, disability, or apparent circumstances.
- Do not turn a stranger into a symbol of a city or social problem without verified context.
- Disclose staging, collaboration, or a posed portrait when known.
- Use neutral, factual language when the context is sensitive.

Primary source: [Magnum Photos Code of Ethics](https://www.magnumphotos.com/magnum-code-of-ethics/)

## Final critique check

- Remove praise that merely makes the image sound important.
- Replace generic interpretation with a visible detail.
- Check whether the strongest claim is supported by the frame.
- Distinguish a fixable crop or tonal problem from missing timing that editing cannot repair.
- Preserve ambiguity in the photograph and accuracy in the critique.
