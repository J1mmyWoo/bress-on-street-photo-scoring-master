# Photobook-based aesthetic profile

This profile distills preferences from the photography and photobooks previously reviewed with the creator. It is a taste layer, not an art-historical ranking or a recipe for imitation.

## Reference library

- Rinko Kawauchi: `Illuminance` and `Hanabi`
- Joel Meyerowitz: `A Question of Color`
- Alex Webb: `The Suffering of Light`
- Henri Cartier-Bresson: work from China, India, portraiture, the Soviet Union, and the Seine
- Gregory Halpern: `ZZYZX` and `Let the Sun Beheaded Be`
- Todd Hido: `Intimate Distance`
- Josef Koudelka: `Wall`

Do not reward a photograph for looking like one of these photographers. Translate the reference library into questions about what the submitted image actually achieves.

## Recurring preferences

### Light as subject

Notice light that actively changes the scene: flare, bloom, haze, reflection, deep shadow, twilight, night illumination, and selective overexposure. Clean exposure is not automatically better. Ask whether light creates structure, attention, atmosphere, or emotional temperature.

### Color with a job

Color should organize attention, create temperature, or carry tension. The reference range includes Kawauchi's pale cyan, pink, and luminous highlights; Meyerowitz's observational color; Webb's saturated contrasts and deep shadow; and Hido's muted night palette. No single grade should become the default.

### Layered and threshold spaces

Value meaningful use of doors, windows, mirrors, foreground blockage, silhouettes, frames within frames, and multiple planes. Complexity must remain readable. A crowded frame with no hierarchy is not equivalent to successful layering.

### Quiet fragments

Ordinary objects, small gestures, empty spaces, distant figures, and atmospheric events can carry a photograph. Do not require peak action in every strong frame. A visual event may be a shift of light, a spatial relation, or an unresolved detail.

### Precision and chance

Cartier-Bresson's influence appears as attention to exact gesture, formal echo, and relationships that lock together at one instant. This can coexist with blur, flare, grain, and partial visibility when those qualities carry the experience of the scene.

### Beauty with unease

The reference library often holds tenderness and threat, attraction and discomfort, or ordinary life and historical pressure in the same sequence. Productive contradiction is preferred to one obvious message.

### People inside environments

People are rarely isolated from place. Background objects, architecture, landscape, and social space should contribute to how a person is read. Direct portraits should preserve dignity and resist invented biography.

### Landscape and infrastructure as evidence

Empty terrain, roads, walls, suburbs, damaged surfaces, and built systems can carry human or political presence without a visible protagonist. Do not dismiss such frames as empty when spatial evidence is specific.

### Sequence awareness

The reference books allow portraits, objects, landscapes, colors, and recurring forms to accumulate meaning through juxtaposition. Judge each frame's standalone quality first, then discuss sequence potential separately instead of using neighboring images to rescue a weak frame.

## Applying the profile

- Keep the absolute numerical score unchanged by taste.
- Report `审美匹配：高 / 中 / 低` separately with one concrete visible reason.
- Use aesthetic fit only as a tiebreaker between photographs with essentially equal absolute scores.
- Let the profile guide which strengths and tensions to notice, not which faults to hide.
- Do not label an image as “Kawauchi-like,” “Webb-like,” “Hido-like,” or similar in the user-facing critique.
- If the user supplies new likes, dislikes, or counterexamples, treat those as newer evidence and update the fit explanation without silently rewriting the absolute rubric.
